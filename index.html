Quick Reference

NightOwl Sleep Calc is a free sleep calculator that helps you plan bedtimes and wake-up times using sleep cycles, sleep latency (how long it takes you to fall asleep), and a wake window (a small flexible range instead of one exact minute). If you’ve ever woken up groggy even after “enough” hours of sleep, timing your wake-up closer to the end of a cycle can help.

This isn’t a medical tool—just practical sleep math. Pick a wake-up time to get recommended bedtimes, or choose “Bedtime now” to see the best wake-up windows based on your settings. Adjust the sliders to match your real-life routine, and use the wake window to give yourself a little breathing room.

How to use this sleep calculator

  1. Choose a mode: “Wake up at…” for bedtime suggestions, or “Bedtime now” for wake-up suggestions.
  2. Set your Tomorrow Goal: Pick a goal (Sharp, Survive, or Recover) to get a tailored cycle recommendation and nap timing.
  3. Set your sleep latency: how long you usually take to fall asleep (many people are around 10–20 minutes).
  4. Set cycle length: 90 minutes is typical, but you can adjust if you tend to run shorter or longer.
  5. Pick a wake window: a small range (like 10–15 minutes) so you can aim for a window, not a single exact time.
  6. Pick an option: choose the cycle count that fits your schedule, then copy or share the plan.

Example 1: If you need to wake up at 7:00 AM and it usually takes you 15 minutes to fall asleep, the calculator will work backward from that time using your selected cycle length and wake window to suggest bedtimes.

Example 2: If you’re looking for the best nap timing, switch to the Nap Calculator and use the current time as your starting point. Pick a short, medium, or long nap to see the best wake-up range.

Example 3: If you’re flying across time zones, the Jet Lag Planner helps you estimate when to sleep and wake based on your destination time. If you’ve had coffee too late, the Caffeine Calculator helps you pick a better cutoff time.

A typical sleep cycle includes lighter sleep, deeper sleep, and REM sleep. Many people feel better waking at the end of a cycle rather than in the middle of one.

Sleep latency is the time between getting into bed and actually falling asleep. It can change based on stress, caffeine, temperature, and screen time.
