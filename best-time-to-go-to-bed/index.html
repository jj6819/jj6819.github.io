Best Time to Go to Bed

The best time to go to bed depends on when you need to wake up, how long it takes you to fall asleep, and how many full sleep cycles you can fit in. A sleep cycle plan won't be perfect every night, but it can make wake-ups feel smoother.

Start with your wake-up time

Instead of guessing a bedtime, work backward from when you need to wake. Choose a cycle count that fits your schedule and gives you a little buffer for sleep latency.

Sleep latency matters (more than people think)

If it takes you 20–30 minutes to fall asleep, your "in bed" time needs to be earlier than your "asleep" time. Adjusting latency makes recommended bedtimes more realistic.

How many cycles should you aim for?

  • 6 cycles: full recharge for many people
  • 5 cycles: a strong, realistic target
  • 4 cycles: minimum option when time is tight

Consistency often matters as much as the perfect number.

Use a wake window to reduce stress

A wake window gives you a range instead of one exact minute. That's helpful because real nights aren't perfectly timed—and it makes the plan easier to stick to.

FAQ

Is it better to sleep earlier or longer?

Most people benefit from both, but consistency and total sleep time usually win.

What if I wake up before my alarm?

That's common. If you wake naturally and feel okay, you can get up—your body may be aligning to a schedule.

How do I stop feeling groggy?

Try shifting to a different cycle option and make sure latency is accurate.
