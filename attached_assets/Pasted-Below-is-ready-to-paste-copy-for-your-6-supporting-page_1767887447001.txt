H2: What is a sleep cycle?
Sleep cycles repeat through the night as your brain moves through lighter sleep, deeper sleep, and REM. A full cycle often averages about 90 minutes, but it can vary by person and by night.

H2: Why waking at cycle boundaries can help
Waking from deep sleep often triggers sleep inertia—the “foggy” feeling. Planning to wake closer to the end of a cycle can reduce the odds of waking at the worst moment.

4) /sleep-latency/

Meta title: Sleep Latency: What It Is + How to Set It | NightOwl
Meta description: Sleep latency is how long it takes to fall asleep. Learn typical ranges and how adjusting latency improves bedtime and wake time calculations.

Page copy

H1: Sleep Latency (Time to Fall Asleep)
Sleep latency is the time it takes you to fall asleep after you get into bed. If your sleep calculator assumes you fall asleep instantly, your bedtimes may be too late. Setting a realistic latency makes your sleep plan much more accurate.

H2: What’s a normal sleep latency?
Many people are around 10–20 minutes, but it varies. Stress, caffeine, screens, naps, and irregular sleep schedules can increase it.

H2: Why latency changes your recommended bedtime
If you want 5 full cycles of sleep but you take 25 minutes to fall asleep, your “in bed” time should be about 25 minutes earlier than the “sleep cycles” math alone.

H2: How to estimate your latency
If you’re not sure, try this for a few nights:
	•	note the time you put your phone down / lights out
	•	estimate when you actually fell asleep
You don’t need perfection—just a realistic average.

H2: Tips that commonly reduce latency
A wind-down routine, dimmer lighting, less late caffeine, and consistent wake times are common helpers. If latency is persistently very long, consider speaking with a professional.

FAQ
Why do I feel tired even if I’m in bed for 8 hours? Time in bed isn’t the same as time asleep. Latency and awakenings matter.
Should I set latency to 0 if I fall asleep fast? You can, but many people still do better with 5–10 minutes.
Does latency change night to night? Yes—use an average that matches most nights.

Meta description: A wake window is a flexible range around a recommended bedtime or wake time. Learn why it helps and how to use it with sleep cycles.

Page copy

H1: Wake Window (Why a Range Beats an Exact Minute)
A wake window is a small flexible range around a recommended bedtime or wake-up time—like 10 to 15 minutes. It makes sleep planning easier because real life is messy, and sleep timing isn’t perfectly precise.

H2: Why a wake window helps
A single “perfect” time can be stressful or unrealistic. A wake window gives you options while keeping the overall cycle timing close enough to be useful.

H2: How to choose your wake window
	•	0–5 minutes: if you want precision
	•	10–15 minutes: most practical
	•	20–30 minutes: maximum flexibility
If you tend to snooze or your routine varies, a slightly bigger window can help.

H2: Wake window vs snoozing
A wake window isn’t the same as snoozing. It’s a planned range so you can pick a time that fits your morning, rather than repeatedly restarting sleep.

FAQ
Does a wake window make the calculator less accurate? It adds flexibility without changing the core cycle math much.
Should I use a wake window for bedtime too? Yes—aiming for a range can reduce stress.
What’s a good default? 10 minutes is a strong default.
